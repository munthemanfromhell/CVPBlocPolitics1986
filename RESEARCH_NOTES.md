# Research and scenario notes

## Point of divergence

This is a provisional mockup timeline, not yet a locked full-mod canon.

The Müller Grand Coalition survives the unemployment-insurance dispute of 1930 by agreeing to a temporary federal subsidy and a limited increase in contributions. The banking crisis of 1931 is met with capital controls, public ownership stakes and reflation rather than prolonged presidential deflation. Unemployment remains severe, but the anti-system right never obtains the electoral plurality necessary to dominate the Reichstag.

Following the 1932 election, the SPD, Centre and liberal parties pass a constitutional amendment requiring a constructive vote of no confidence and limiting the duration of emergency decrees. The NSDAP fractures between Hitler and the Strasser organisation after a failed attempt to overthrow the Prussian government. Hindenburg dies in office in 1934 and Otto Braun becomes the first Social Democratic president elected under the stabilised constitution.

## Long-term development

The Republic gradually becomes more federal and parliamentary. The Centre Party reorganises as the interconfessional Christian People's Party during the 1950s. The Bavarian People's Party remains autonomous. The DDP and DVP traditions eventually consolidate into separate republican-democratic and national-liberal parties.

From the 1960s onward, electoral coordination hardens into two blocs. The SPD-led Left Bloc joins social democrats, left socialists, social liberals and Christian reformers. The CVP-led People's Bloc joins Christian democrats, Bavarian regionalists, national liberals, conservative regional lists and the republicanised DNVP.

A 1971 reform fixes the Reichstag at 600 seats and uses state proportional allocation with national adjustment seats. The blocs are not legal parties, but joint candidates and parliamentary discipline make them increasingly difficult to escape.

## 1986 election

The 1982 election returns a narrow Left Bloc majority under Hans-Jochen Vogel. Its government is weakened by industrial recession, disputes over federal equalisation, the decline of coal and steel, and opposition to a nuclear reprocessing complex in Silesia. The RDP withdraws after the second failed budget in late 1985. President Hildegard Hamm-Brücher dissolves the Reichstag and calls a snap election for March 1986.

The CVP campaigns under Heinrich Windelen. The result gives the People's Bloc 319 of 600 seats, but the DNVP's 38 deputies make the alliance politically contentious. The player may preserve the bloc, replace the DNVP with the RDP, or form a Grand Coalition with the SPD.

## Historical personalities used

Real German politicians are used as raw biographical material, but their party careers and offices diverge substantially from real history. Heinrich Windelen was selected because his Silesian Catholic background fits an unpartitioned German Republic especially well. Hans-Jochen Vogel, Hildegard Hamm-Brücher, Burkhard Hirsch, Alfred Dregger and Oskar Lafontaine occupy plausible but alternate institutional roles.

## Provisional items

The international system, eastern borders, presidential chronology, state constitutions and complete post-1928 election history remain open for revision before any full mod is attempted.
